Verdict: This is a well-crafted design-studio landing page that has not yet been built as a medical-AI buyer page. The demo's "AI proposes — the radiologist disposes" framing and source-linked summary are genuinely strong; the failure is that a radiologist cannot see chest imaging or a credible clinical claim in the first screen. More urgently, both conversion paths are broken: "Book a demo" goes to a Google/GitHub login, and "Try for Free" did nothing in two fresh anonymous sessions.
What's working
- The demo's safety framing is excellent: "The AI proposes — the radiologist disposes," "Draft queued to Dr. Ibekwe — not signed," and per-finding source chips (FILM, CHART, LABS, NOTE).
- The multimodal story is made concrete, not abstract: one screen shows patient banner, vitals/labs, image, findings, AI summary, and document extraction.
- Cohesive visual system: restrained teal/ink/white palette, consistent hairline rules, strong display type, generous spacing.
- Mobile genuinely reflows (verified at 390 px with device emulation): the demo stacks to one column, no horizontal overflow.
- Some accessibility basics are in place: lang="en", one h1, logical h2/h3, aria-labels on icon buttons, role="img" with label on the X-ray SVG, global prefers-reduced-motion handling.
Section-by-section review
1. First impression (5-second test) — 3/10
Evidence: Hero reads "Healthcare / Intelligence With Clarity" over a painted mountain-lake landscape, with one button, "Try for Free."
Problem: A visitor can infer "healthcare AI," but not chest X-ray/CT, not radiologists, not decision support, and not what to do. The background signals wellness retreat, not radiology. The title tag is more specific than the page.
Fix: Lead with chest imaging and the user ("for radiologists"), show a real product crop or chest study, and make the primary action a clinical walkthrough.
2. Hero — 3/10
Evidence: H1 is brand-led and category-generic. Subheadline: "One intelligent workspace for medical images, reports, and health data…". One CTA in hero; header has "Sign in" and "Book a demo" (both → /login). Background is /background.png, 1344×768, 1.9 MB.
Problem: No specificity, no proof, no imaging, two competing action models (self-serve "free" vs. sales demo), and the only hero CTA is non-functional in an anonymous session.
Fix: Specific H1 + one-sentence outcome + two CTAs ("Book a clinical walkthrough" primary, "See the 2-minute read" secondary) + a clinically relevant visual.
3. Messaging and copy — 4/10
Evidence: "Zero friction," "One intelligent workspace," "AI Understands," "Clinical Insights," "evidence-informed insights and recommendations." The product is described as chest X-ray/CT in the meta title, but as generic "medical images, reports, and health data" on-page; the demo shows only a CXR and no CT. "02 / Product" appears without a "01."
Problem: Positioning drifts between chest subspecialty tool and horizontal healthcare-AI platform. Generic claims and "Zero friction" are unsupported. The best copy on the site is the meta description, not the hero.
Fix: Commit to chest X-ray/CT for radiologists. Use the meta line ("fusing imaging, reports and clinical text into one clear read") as the hero. Remove absolutes; label steps consistently.
4. Trust and credibility — 1/10
Evidence: No logos, testimonials, validation metrics, publications, advisors, regulatory status, or company/contact details. Footer "Security/About/Journal/Contact/Radiology playbooks/API reference/FHIR & DICOM" all link to #top; /about, /contact, /security return 404.
Problem: Hospital buyers and radiologists have zero verifiable reasons to believe the claims. Dead trust links are worse than absent ones.
Fix: Add a proof block only with verifiable facts: validation design and N, external test set, published source, pilot sites (with permission), named clinical advisors, and a real contact. If you don't have these, run an honest "design partner / pilot" program instead of implying maturity.
5. Compliance and safety language — 2/10 (flags, not legal advice)
Evidence: No intended-use statement, regulatory status, or decision-support disclaimer anywhere. The demo states "Most consistent with community-acquired pneumonia — empiric coverage started," shows "conf 0.94," and displays a named patient ("Okafor, Roland," MRN 88-19344) with no "synthetic" label. The feature card claims the product can "generate evidence-informed insights and recommendations." /login says users agree to "terms and privacy policy," but /terms and /privacy are 404.
Problem: A specific diagnosis plus a treatment step ("empiric coverage started") can look like a diagnostic/treatment recommendation rather than decision support — potentially outside FDA CDS exemption criteria and requiring clearance. Unlabeled realistic identifiers create a synthetic-vs-real PHI ambiguity. "Secure by Design… encryption" is unsubstantiated, and missing terms/privacy is a live legal/compliance gap.
Fix (flag, not legal advice): Add intended use, indications, limitations, and market-specific regulatory status. Replace diagnostic/treatment phrasing in the demo with "candidate finding — radiologist review required," remove or justify the confidence score, and label all demo data "Synthetic patient data for illustration." Publish privacy, terms, and a security page before collecting any data.

9. Conversion — 2/10

10. Accessibility — 5/10

Problem: Small targets and micro-text hurt low-vision and touch users. Hero subheadline uses #52525b over a variable landscape image; contrast is not measured but likely dips below AA in light areas. Findings are announced as buttons but cannot be activated by keyboard.
Fix: Raise tap targets to ≥44 px, demo text to ≥12 px, add keyboard activation to findings, verify contrast over the hero (or add a solid scrim), and add visible focus styles to nav/footer links.

Fix: Server-render the marketing route without the auth check; add static H1/content. Convert the hero image to AVIF/WebP (<200 KB) or remove it; preload only if it remains the LCP. Add OG/Twitter tags, canonical, robots.txt, sitemap.xml, and Organization/SoftwareApplication JSON-LD.

Two subheadlines

2. "For radiology teams: a source-linked summary of the study and the patient context. You review, edit, and sign. Nothing is auto-diagnosed."
Primary CTA label
"Book a 20-minute clinical walkthrough" (secondary: "See the 2-minute read")
"How it works" rewrite
1. Connect the study — Send chest X-rays/CT and prior reports via DICOM/FHIR, or upload a study. ClarityCXR pulls the relevant chart, labs, and priors.
2. Review the fused read — Findings appear on the image, linked to the source text that supports them. Accept, edit, or reject each one.
3. Sign with your judgment — Export to your reporting workflow with your sign-off and an audit trail. The AI proposes; the radiologist decides.
Missing-content checklist for a medical-AI page
- Intended use, indications, contraindications, and limitations
- Regulatory status by market (FDA/CE/MDR), with clearance numbers if held
- Decision-support disclaimer ("not a diagnosis; for use by qualified clinicians")
- Clinical validation: study design, sample size, endpoints, external test set, published source
- Named pilot sites/case studies (with permission) or an honest design-partner program
- Security: HIPAA/BAA, GDPR, encryption in transit/at rest, audit logs, RBAC, data residency, retention, subprocessors, SOC 2 if held
- Data handling: synthetic/de-identified demo statement; whether customer data is used for training
- Integrations: DICOM, PACS, worklist, FHIR/HL7, deployment model (cloud/on-prem/VPC)
- Pricing or pilot terms; procurement/security questionnaire
- Team and clinical advisors; company contact and address
- Privacy policy, terms, DPA, cookie notice
- FAQ (regulatory, integration, data, workflow)
- Closing CTA with a real form; accessibility statement; status page (optional)
